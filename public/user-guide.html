DiscoveryDesk User Guide

A quick guide to running a product discovery from first persona to signed-off business case.

What is DiscoveryDesk?

DiscoveryDesk is a structured workspace for product discovery — the work that happens before anyone decides what to build. Instead of freeform templates, it walks you through six proven frameworks (Jobs-to-be-Done, Value Proposition Canvas, Business Model Canvas) as sequential phases, each producing an output your stakeholders review, comment on, and sign off. It is not a backlog manager or project tracker — it is where the team decides whether to build something, and for whom.

Getting in

Accounts & sign-in

  • Invited collaborators sign in with the email and password they created when opening an invite link. There is no open signup — accounts are created only through an invite link someone shared with you.
  • Guest access (testing phase): the sign-in page offers Join as guest — no email needed, and each guest gets their own sandbox workspace.

Roles

RoleHow you get itWhat you can do
Owner / BA Creates the Discovery Everything: edit all phases, invite collaborators, submit phases for review, approve, reopen, delete
Stakeholder Invite link (Stakeholder role) View, comment, Approve or Request Changes on phases. Cannot edit phase content, submit phases for review, or invite others
Guest Share link, no login (testing phase) View, comment, and sign off under a display name; can also submit phases for review. Sees only the one shared Discovery
Note: guest access via share link is temporary — it exists for the current testing phase. After testing, everyone will sign in with email and password via invite links.

Creating and sharing a Discovery

  1. On the Discoveries list page, click Create Discovery, give it a name, and click Create.
  2. To bring people in, use the Invite collaborators button (top bar), pick the role the link should grant, and click Copy invite link. Share the link through your own channel (Slack, email, chat) — recipients create their account through the link and land directly in the Discovery.
  3. For solo work you want someone to peek at without an account, copy the Guest link instead — anyone with it can view, comment, and sign off under a display name.

The six phases

Every Discovery follows the same sequence. Each phase builds on the ones before it:

  1. Persona — Understand the customer you are designing for (functional, emotional, and social jobs, plus desired outcomes).
  2. Pain/Gain — Capture and score the customer's pains and gains. Pains are scored on severity, frequency, and business impact; the ranking is calculated for you.
  3. Value Proposition — Map your value proposition against the customer profile. The Customer Profile pre-fills from Phases 1–2, and unaddressed pains are flagged as gaps.
  4. Business Model — Describe how the business creates, delivers, and captures value across the nine canvas blocks. A gross-margin indicator appears when you enter numeric revenue and cost figures.
  5. Vision — State the product vision (one sentence) and the problem statement (who, what, when, where, why). Your top pains from Phase 2 are surfaced as prompts.
  6. Business Case — Define success metrics, estimated investment, projected impact, and key assumptions (tagged Confirmed / Unconfirmed / Unknown).

Working through a phase

  1. Fill it in. Guidance text is shown inline for each section. Everything saves automatically — close the tab and your work is still there.
  2. Submit it. When the output is complete, click Submit for Review. The phase state becomes In review.
  3. Get sign-off. Stakeholders (and you, as Owner) review the output and either click Approve or Request Changes with a required comment. A change request sends the phase back to Draft with the comment visible.
Sequential gating: a phase can't be submitted until the phase before it has been signed off. Locked phases in the stepper show "Complete <phase> first."

Comments

Every phase has a comment thread. Click Add comment to leave feedback. Comments form a flat list and can be marked Resolved / Unresolved by their author or the Owner; use Show resolved / Hide resolved to manage the noise.

Revising an approved phase

Need to change something after sign-off? The Owner can use Reopen Phase — this creates a new revision and discards the existing sign-offs on that phase, so use it deliberately.

Finishing: approving the Discovery

When all six phases are approved, click Mark Approved in the top bar. The Discovery becomes Approved and is locked against editing — any further change requires reopening a specific phase. The approval record (who, when) is kept with the Discovery.

Good to know

  • There are no email notifications. Collaborators see new states and comments when they open the app or when the page refreshes itself (~every 10 seconds). As the BA, tell your stakeholders when their review is waiting.
  • Deletion is permanent. Deleting a Discovery removes all six phases, their outputs, and all collaborator invites. This cannot be undone.
  • Names are editable. Click the Discovery name in the top bar to rename it at any time.
  • Summary view. The Summary link in the top bar shows a one-glance Executive Summary: the problem statement, product vision, and each phase's sign-off status.
